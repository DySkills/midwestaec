# MIAEC Editorial & Content Architecture Guide

> **Midwest Architecture, Engineering & Construction (MIAEC)**  
> *Target Region: Climate Zones 4 & 5 (IL, IN, IA, KS, MI, MN, MO, ND, NE, OH, SD, WI)*  
> *Primary Mission: Objective Building Science Authority, High-Intent Contractor Referrals, Software Funnel*

---

## 1. The Editorial Persona

All MIAEC content is written through a clearly defined editorial perspective:

- **Identity & Background:** 35-year-old male from northern Missouri. NAAB-accredited Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch), raised in a family of construction tradesmen, passionate about materials physical chemistry and AEC technology.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Objective voice grounded in jobsite reality**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "When I was a kid swinging a hammer..."). The background informs the technical depth and practical lens, not personal storytelling.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Plainspoken engineering. Precise technical concepts (*latent heat, convective loops, thermal bridging, stack-effect depressurization, vapor permeability*) followed immediately by straightforward jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and matter-of-fact. Never writes angry rants or marketing hype. Lets ASTM test data, thermodynamic physics, and public utility commission dockets quietly expose predatory sales tactics or bad construction practices.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Midwest Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1968 brick ranches in St. Louis or Kansas City, 1920s uninsulated two-stories in Des Moines, 1970s split-levels in northern Missouri, Chicago brick bungalows).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Subtle, ongoing appreciation for physical chemistry and performance (e.g., why stone wool repels bulk water, how polyamide smart retarders open at 60% RH, why borate cellulose halts convective looping at -10°F).

## 2. The 4 Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on MIAEC must strictly follow one of four standardized structural formats:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          4 CONTENT FORMATS                             │
├─────────────────┬─────────────────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────┤
│    FEATURES     │     REPORTS     │    LISTICLES     │    TUTORIALS    │
│  (Deep Dives /  │  (Fact-First /  │  (Tips, Lists &  │  (Step-by-Step  │
│   Archetypes)   │  Tech Analysis) │  Checklists)     │   Processes)    │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┴──────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative pieces, building archetype profiles, seasonal analyses, and human-interest stories.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Styled callout container with executive summary text followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately.
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never use large dense blocks of text.
- **Lead Guidelines:**
  - **For Increasing Costs / Utility Spikes:** **Descriptive Leads** (paint the physical scene: sub-zero midnight, furnace cycling, cold draft at the ankles, the utility bill on the counter).
  - **For Building Archetypes:** **Staccato Leads** (short, punchy rhythmic phrases: *Two stories. Continuous pine studs. Zero fire stops. Across Des Moines, Chicago, and Milwaukee...*).

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of energy codes, mechanical equipment, and envelope performance.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Data Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Quick Summary Box** with verified performance numbers.
  4. **Bold Section Titles** (`## Section`).
  5. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph**.

### Format 3: Listicles (Tips, Comparisons & Checklists)
- **Role:** High-shareability, high-intent comparison shopping, and audit preparation.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Contrast Hook $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Mini-Sections $\rightarrow$ Jobsite Application.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Quick Summary Box** outlining the comparative verdict.
  3. **Numbered Items (1 to 5, 1 to 10):** Each item gets a bold mini-heading, 2–3 sentence explanation, and a bulleted jobsite tip.

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Instructions & Processes)
- **Role:** Practical diagnostic or execution steps for homeowners and tradesmen already on site.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Objective & Safety $\rightarrow$ Required Tools/Materials $\rightarrow$ Sequential Numbered Steps $\rightarrow$ Verification Test.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Quick Summary Box** with difficulty rating, required time, and tools.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** Step 1, Step 2, Step 3 with clear photographic or diagrammatic references.

## 5. Technical Rigor & Sourcing Requirements

No article may make an empirical performance claim without citing at least two of the following authoritative sources:
1. **ASTM International Standards:** e.g., ASTM E779 (Air Leakage Fan Depressurization), ASTM C739 (Loose-Fill Cellulose), ASTM E119 (Fire Tests), ASTM E96 (Water Vapor Transmission).
2. **National Laboratories:** Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL Building Technologies Research Center), Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL).
3. **Engineering Handbooks:** ASHRAE Handbook of Fundamentals (specifically Chapter 16 for infiltration and stack dynamics).
4. **Model Energy Codes:** International Energy Conservation Code (IECC 2021/2024), International Residential Code (IRC).
5. **State Utility Dockets:** Public Service Commission and Commerce Commission formal rate case filings (Ameren, Spire, Nicor, ComEd, MidAmerican, Evergy).

## 6. Visual & Technical Diagram Style Guide

All diagrams across MIAEC must maintain a cohesive architectural whitepaper aesthetic:

- **Style:** Clean, architectural cross-section linework drawn over a faint, light gray-blue blueprint grid background (`#FBFBFC` canvas with `#E2E8F0` gridlines).
- **Color System (60-30-10):**
  - **Dominant Linework (60%):** Deep Navy (`#161F83`) for primary walls, structural framing, foundations, joists, and rafters.
  - **Secondary Flow & Air (30%):** Steel Blue (`#3274AE`) for cold outdoor air infiltration, bulk water, and neutral lines.
  - **Thermal Exfiltration & Accent (10%):** Crimson Red (`#C42927`) for warm buoyant air movement, heat loss bypasses, and critical failure zones.
- **Typography in Diagrams:** Clean sans-serif technical annotations (e.g., *Newsreader* or *Plus Jakarta Sans* / *JetBrains Mono*) with clear leader lines pointing to exact construction assemblies (e.g., "Mud Sill", "Rim Joist", "Top Plate Bypass", "Neutral Pressure Plane").
- **Carousels & Comparators:** When demonstrating contrasting building archetypes (e.g., Two-Story vs. Single-Story Ranch, or Solid Masonry vs. Veneer Brick), diagrams must be embedded in an interactive carousel with tabbed archetype pills so readers can toggle between housing types seamlessly.
