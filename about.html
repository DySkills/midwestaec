About MIAEC

Engineering Standards for the Midwest Envelope

MIAEC (Midwest AEC) was established to solve a critical regional problem: generic, national building advice does not survive a Midwestern winter followed by a Midwestern summer.

Why Climate Zones 4 & 5 Require Different Physics

In hot-humid Florida, moisture always pushes into the home. In arctic Canada, moisture always pushes out. But across the American Midwest—spanning Missouri, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Iowa, Michigan, Wisconsin, and Kansas—the vapor drive completely reverses every six months.

Installing an impermeable 6-mil poly vapor barrier behind drywall in a Chicago or St. Louis home causes inward summer moisture to condense and breed mold. Conversely, failing to install continuous exterior insulation causes interior sheathing temperatures to drop below 45°F during January polar vortexes, rotting wall framing.

Our Focus: Remodels, R-Value & Energy Resilience

Midwest cities contain tens of thousands of historic brick bungalows, post-war ranches, and aging housing stock. With utility rates climbing dramatically, homeowners, flippers, and trades need practical, field-verified answers to:

  • Insulating Mass Brick Masonry: How to insulate 100-year-old brick from the inside without causing destructive winter freeze/thaw spalling.
  • High-ROI Thermal Retrofits: Why dense-pack attic air-sealing and rim joist spray foam deliver 5x the energy savings of expensive replacement windows.
  • Cold-Climate Inverter Mechanicals: How modern variable-speed heat pumps maintain 100% heating capacity down to -15°F while continuously managing summer humidity.

Specification Standards & Independence

MIAEC is an independent technical publisher. Every specification published on this platform is benchmarked against recognized consensus testing standards:

• ASTM E1745: Under-Slab Water Vapor Retarders
• ASTM C518: Steady-State Thermal Transmission (True R-Value)
• ASTM E96: Water Vapor Permeance of Materials
• ASTM E779 / E1827: Blower Door Air Leakage Standards
• IECC 2024 / ASHRAE 90.1: Energy Conservation Building Codes
