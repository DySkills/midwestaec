Keeping Pests Out of a Midwest Home: Gaps, Moisture and the Ground Around the House

Mice, carpenter ants and termites all get in the same way: through small gaps, damp wood and soil or mulch against the house. Here is how to close those paths, with the numbers DOE and University of Minnesota Extension recommend.

By Midwest AEC Building Science • Published November 2026 • DOE Building America / UMN Extension
Quick Summary

Sprays treat pests that are already inside. Keeping them out is mostly a building job: seal gaps a mouse can fit through, fix the damp wood carpenter ants love, and keep soil, mulch and branches back from the house. The same work also cuts drafts and moisture problems, so it pays off twice.

  • Seal exterior gaps larger than a quarter inch.
  • Fix moisture and replace damp, decayed wood.
  • Keep 2 feet of gravel or pavers, not wood mulch, against the house.
  • Keep siding at least 6 inches above the soil.

Every fall, as nights get cold, Midwest houses get visitors: mice moving in from the fields, cluster flies and boxelder bugs looking for a warm wall, and in spring, swarms of winged carpenter ants. Most come in through the same weak spots.

Seal the Gaps

How small a gap matters?

DOE's Building America Solution Center says mice can squeeze through openings as small as a quarter inch, and rats through half an inch. It recommends sealing exterior holes and joints larger than a quarter inch or covering them with quarter-inch metal mesh.

Where are the gaps?

Check where pipes, wires, cable and dryer vents go through the wall, the gap at the sill plate where the house sits on the foundation, garage door seals, and roof and soffit vents. DOE recommends quarter-inch metal screening on roof vents.

What should I seal them with?

Use materials pests can't chew through for larger gaps: metal mesh, sheet metal, mortar or concrete patch. Sealant or foam alone works for small cracks, but mice will chew through soft foam.

The same sealing also stops drafts, so it's worth doing at the rim joist while you're there.

Fix the Moisture

Carpenter ants don't eat wood, but they nest in it, and they pick damp wood. University of Minnesota Extension says carpenter ant nests in homes are found in "moist or decayed wood," and that preventing them means eliminating high moisture conditions and replacing moisture-damaged wood (Hahn and Kells, reviewed 2024).

Where should I look?

Look around leaky windows and doors, under a dripping roof edge or ice dam area, at a wet rim joist, around plumbing leaks and under bathrooms. UMN lists coarse sawdust, steady sightings of 20 or more worker ants indoors, and winged ants indoors from late winter through spring as signs of a nest.

What else helps?

UMN recommends storing firewood as far from the house as possible and trimming branches so they don't touch the roof or eaves.

Manage the Ground Around the House

DOE's Building America Solution Center gives specific clearances:

How high should the siding be?

At least 6 inches between siding and soil, with 8 to 12 inches preferred. That keeps soil-dwelling insects, including termites, from having a hidden path into the wall, and lets you see any mud tubes.

What goes against the foundation?

About 2 feet of crushed rock, pea gravel, concrete or pavers instead of wood mulch, and never wood chips piled against wood siding.

How close can plants and trees be?

Nothing planted within 18 inches of the house, at least 2 feet between shrubs and the building, and tree limbs at least 6 feet from the walls, or 10 feet if squirrels are a problem.

What about water?

DOE recommends sloping the ground away from the foundation at half an inch per foot for at least 10 feet, and sending downspouts at least 5 feet away. Dry soil next to the house is less inviting to termites and ants.

What About Termites?

Eastern subterranean termites live in much of the lower and central Midwest. Wood touching soil, decks and fences built tight to the house, and wood mulch against the foundation give them a hidden way in.

DOE recommends at least 6 inches of clearance between the house and wooden decks or fences so you can inspect, and termite shields at the top of foundation walls. If you see mud tubes on the foundation, call a licensed pest professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

How small a hole can a mouse get through?

About a quarter inch. DOE's Building America Solution Center recommends sealing exterior holes and joints larger than a quarter inch, or covering them with quarter-inch metal mesh. Rats can get through openings as small as half an inch.

What attracts carpenter ants to a house?

Moist or decayed wood. University of Minnesota Extension says carpenter ant nests in homes are found in moist or decayed wood, and that eliminating high moisture conditions and replacing moisture-damaged wood is the way to prevent them.

How far should mulch be from the house?

DOE guidance recommends a perimeter of about 2 feet of crushed rock, pea gravel, concrete or pavers instead of wood mulch, and never letting wood chips pile against wood siding. It also recommends keeping at least 6 inches, ideally 8 to 12, between siding and soil.

How far should trees be from the house?

DOE guidance suggests keeping tree limbs at least 6 feet from the walls, or 10 feet if squirrels are a problem. University of Minnesota Extension also recommends trimming branches so they don't touch the roof or eaves.
