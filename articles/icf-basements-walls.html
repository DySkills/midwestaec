ICF Basements: How Insulated Concrete Forms Perform in a Midwest Winter

An ICF wall is concrete poured between two layers of foam, so the insulation is built in on both sides. Here is what a DOE-funded cold-climate test house measured, and what it cost.

By Midwest AEC Building Science • Published November 2026 • DOE Building America / BSC BSD-103
Quick Summary

Insulated concrete forms are hollow foam blocks stacked into a wall and filled with concrete. The foam stays in place, so the finished wall has insulation on both sides of the concrete. A DOE-funded test house in a cold climate measured an R-22 ICF foundation and very tight air sealing, at a cost premium that paid back slowly. ICF makes the most sense for new houses and additions, not as a fix for an existing basement.

  • 5 inches of total foam gave an R-22 wall in the test house.
  • The foam is not waterproofing. You still need a membrane and drainage.
  • The ICF foundation cost about $2,500 more than a framed R-13 wall in that house.
  • For an existing basement, foam board inside the wall is usually the practical route.

A regular poured basement wall is just concrete, and concrete conducts heat well. Building Science Corporation's basement guide (BSD-103) cites research that basements can account for "upwards of 20 percent" of a house's heating and cooling load. ICF is one way to put the insulation where it's needed from the start.

How Is an ICF Wall Built?

The forms are blocks made of two foam panels, usually expanded polystyrene (EPS), held apart by plastic ties. Crews stack them on the footing, add rebar, and pour concrete into the middle. The foam never comes off: it becomes the insulation on the outside and the inside of the wall.

What Did a Cold-Climate Test House Show?

DOE's Building America program built and studied a test house with ICF walls in climate zone 5, the same zone as much of Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Michigan and Nebraska (Mallay and Wiehagen, Home Innovation Research Labs, 2014).

How well did it insulate?

The ICF foundation walls had 5 inches of total EPS and were rated R-22, with a calculated R-21.5 for the clear wall. That beats the R-15 Building America recommended for foundation walls in zone 5.

How tight was it?

The finished house tested at 1.10 air changes per hour at 50 pascals on a blower door, well under the project's goal of 2.0. Much of that comes from the continuous concrete and foam having very few seams to leak.

Did the concrete's mass help?

A little. Simulations showed the ICF walls used about 9 percent less energy on average than a frame wall of similar R-value, and up to 30 percent less during some very cold hours. The report calls the difference modest.

Does ICF Stop Moisture?

Not on its own. EPS foam is a vapor retarder, so the concrete inside dries slowly. The Building America report found the test house's EPS forms measured about 0.8 to 1.5 perms at 2.5 inches thick, and it recommends coverings that let the wall dry both ways.

Below grade, the test house still needed full waterproofing: a peel-and-stick membrane on the foundation, a dimpled drainage membrane from the footing up to grade, perimeter drainage and a capillary break on the footing. The basement also measured 60 percent relative humidity during one humid visit, likely from the new concrete curing.

What Does It Cost?

In the test house, the ICF foundation cost $2,528 more than a framed foundation wall insulated to R-13. With ICF walls above grade as well, the whole house cost $18,348 more than standard construction. The report estimated about $1,018 a year in utility savings, for a simple payback of about 18 years.

Those are one house's numbers in one market, but they give a realistic picture: ICF costs more up front and pays back over a long time. The case is strongest if you also value the quieter, tighter house and the finished basement walls that don't need a separate insulation job.

Is ICF Right for Your Project?

Are you building new or adding on?

That's where ICF fits best, since the foundation is being built anyway.

Are you finishing an existing basement?

You can't easily turn an existing wall into ICF. BSD-103 recommends rigid foam or closed-cell spray foam directly against the concrete instead, such as 2 inches of XPS for about R-10.

Frequently Asked Questions

What R-value is an ICF basement wall?

It depends on the foam thickness. In a DOE Building America cold-climate test house, ICF walls with 5 inches of total EPS foam were rated R-22, and a calculation gave R-21.5 for the clear wall.

Do ICF basements still need waterproofing?

Yes. The foam is not a waterproofing layer. The Building America test house used a peel-and-stick membrane on the foundation and a dimpled drainage membrane from the footing to grade, along with perimeter drainage.

How much more does an ICF foundation cost?

In the Building America test house, the ICF foundation cost $2,528 more than a framed R-13 wall. The whole house, with ICF walls above grade as well, cost $18,348 more than standard construction, with a simple payback of about 18 years.

Are ICF walls more energy efficient than wood frame walls of the same R-value?

Somewhat. Simulations for the Building America test house showed ICF walls used about 9 percent less energy on average than a frame wall of similar R-value, and up to 30 percent less during some very cold hours, because of the concrete's thermal mass.
