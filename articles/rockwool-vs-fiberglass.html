Rockwool Stone Wool vs. Pink Fiberglass Batts: Real-World R-Value, Moisture & Fire Resistance

By MIAEC Building Science • Published 2026-10-18 • ASTM C665 vs. ASTM C612
Executive Summary: Walk into any Midwest Lowe's, Home Depot, or lumberyard, and the insulation aisle is dominated by two main choices for wall cavities: traditional pink/yellow fiberglass batts and dense gray stone mineral wool (Rockwool Comfortbatt). Fiberglass costs roughly 30% to 40% less on the shelf. However, building science laboratory testing reveals that fiberglass is an air-permeable filter that derates in R-value when air leaks through it or when damp. Stone wool is hydrophobic, fireproof to 2,150°F, and delivers true rated performance over decades.

1. How Fiber Structure Dictates Thermal Performance

Explain manufacturing: Fiberglass is spun molten glass fibers bound with acrylic resins. Stone wool is volcanic basalt rock melted at 2,900°F and spun into high-density mineral fibers. Stone wool is roughly 3 times denser than standard fiberglass batts (2.0–2.5 lbs/cu.ft vs 0.6–0.8 lbs/cu.ft).

2. Real-World R-Value: Laboratory Box vs. Jobsite Drafts

Explain ASTM C518 testing: Rated R-value is measured in a hermetically sealed laboratory box with zero air movement. In real Midwest homes with air leakage, low-density fiberglass batts allow convective airflow loops within the wall cavity, derating true thermal resistance by 20% to 35%. Dense mineral wool blocks internal convection, maintaining true R-value.

4. Moisture Durability in Humid Midwest Summers

Detail mold resistance: Stone wool contains zero organic material for mold to feed on. If minor plumbing leaks or exterior condensation wet the stone wool, it dries out completely without losing its loft or structural thickness, unlike fiberglass which bunches into useless wet clumps.

5. Contractor & DIY Installation Advice

Cutting and fitting: Stone wool cuts cleanly with a serrated bread knife or insulation saw around electrical boxes and plumbing pipes. Fiberglass tears and leaves air gaps.
