Below-Slab & Rim Joist Foam Specs: XPS vs. EPS vs. Closed-Cell Spray Foam

By MIAEC Building Science • Published 2026-10-18 • ASTM C578 (Rigid Cellular Foam)
Quick Summary & Essential Takeaways

Executive Summary: When insulating unconditioned foundation areas—such as sub-slab gravel beds, basement frost walls, and rim joists—insulation materials face continuous dampness, soil chemistry, and extreme compression. Using the wrong foam leads to moisture absorption, crushed boards, and loss of R-value. Builders and remodelers frequently debate extruded polystyrene (blue/pink XPS), high-density expanded polystyrene (white/gray EPS), and 2-pound closed-cell spray foam. Here is how they compare under ASTM engineering standards.

1. The Hostile Subterranean Environment

Explain underground conditions: Ground soil in the Midwest remains at 85% to 100% relative humidity year-round. Concrete slabs exert between 15 and 40 pounds per square inch (psi) of dead and live load across the insulation. Insulation installed under concrete must resist compressive creep and water absorption indefinitely.

2. Water Absorption Reality: XPS vs. Type IX EPS

Explain long-term field studies from Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). While new XPS initially resists moisture well, its dense skin absorbs moisture slowly over 15 years in wet soils, eventually holding up to 18% moisture by volume and losing up to 45% of its R-value. High-density EPS (Type IX, 25 psi) drains moisture faster and stabilizes its R-value over decades.

4. Why Closed-Cell Spray Foam Dominates the Rim Joist

Detail the rim joist application: Because rim joists feature irregular angles, anchor bolts, and wiring, rigid board foam requires tedious cutting and edge caulking. Two inches of closed-cell spray foam provides an airtight, R-13 vapor-impermeable seal that bonds directly to the wood rim, concrete sill, and subfloor in a single application.

5. Contractor Checklist for Below-Slab Foam Placement

Installation rules: Laying foam sheets over a compacted 4-inch crushed washed gravel bed. Staggering board seams and taping joints before laying the 15-mil Class A vapor barrier directly on top of the foam.
