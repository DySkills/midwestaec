# Flat Roof Drainage on Midwest Additions: EPDM vs. TPO, Ponding Water and Scupper Sizing

> **Quick Summary:** In residential construction across the Midwest, flat and low-slope roofs (slopes under 2:12) over porch enclosures, garage additions, and modern master suite expansions represent one of the highest insurance claim categories. Homeowners often discover standing ponds of water that linger for days after heavy rains. Standing water accelerates membrane ultraviolet oxidation, degrades seam adhesives, increases structural dead load deflection, and freezes into heavy ice slabs during sub-zero cold snaps. Eliminating low-slope roof leaks requires designing for positive drainage and selecting the appropriate single-ply membrane chemistry.
>
> - The International Residential Code (IRC Section R905.12) mandates a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot (2% slope) for positive drainage on single-ply roof membranes.
> - Ponding water is defined by the National Roofing Contractors Association (NRCA) as water that remains on a roof deck 48 hours after precipitation under conditions conducive to drying.
> - Black 60-mil EPDM rubber provides superior low-temperature elasticity down to -45 deg F and melts snow faster via solar absorption, making it ideal for northern cold climates (Zone 6A).
> - White 60-mil TPO offers heat-welded monolithic seams that are 3 to 4 times stronger than taped EPDM seams, with high solar reflectance that reduces summer cooling loads in mixed-humid zones (Zone 4A).

## The Physics of Ponding Water and Structural Deflection

Flat roofs rarely fail from rain that drains off immediately; they fail from water that stays. When water ponds in a low spot, it adds significant dead weight to the roof framing:
* Water weighs 62.4 pounds per cubic foot.
* A 1-inch deep pond across a 10-foot by 10-foot area weighs 520 pounds.
* A 3-inch deep pond across that same bay adds 1,560 pounds of concentrated dead load.

This weight causes the wood rafters or engineered trusses to deflect downward. Deflection deepens the depression, which collects more water in a dangerous positive feedback loop known as structural ponding instability. During sub-zero January cold snaps, this pooled water freezes, expanding into a thick ice lens that exerts lateral hydrostatic stress against parapet flashings and seam edges.

## IRC R905.12 Positive Drainage & Scupper Hydraulic Sizing

Achieving positive drainage on an existing flat roof with level rafters requires tapered polyisocyanurate insulation panels.
* **Tapered Crickets:** Diamond-shaped tapered polyiso crickets sloped at 1/2 inch per foot are installed between scuppers or drains to divert water laterally toward the drainage outlets.
* **Thru-Wall Scuppers (IRC R903.4.1):** When roof drainage flows through parapet walls, scuppers must be sized based on total tributary roof area. For a standard 500 sq ft addition, the primary scupper must be at least 4 inches wide by 4 inches tall.
* **Secondary Overflow Relief:** Building code requires an independent overflow scupper or secondary drain positioned exactly 2 inches above the primary drain elevation. If the primary scupper is blocked by autumn leaves or winter ice, the overflow scupper prevents catastrophic structural ponding.

## Step-by-Step Low-Slope Roof Replacement Protocol

1. **Tear Off to Structural Deck:** Strip failed multi-ply asphalt roofing, rotted fiberboard, and wet insulation down to the bare plywood or OSB roof deck. Replace any deflected, rotted, or water-damaged sheathing.
2. **Install Air & Vapor Barrier:** Adhere a self-adhering vapor retarder sheet (such as Soprema Sopravapor) directly to the wood deck. This prevents warm interior humidity from migrating into the cold insulation core.
3. **Lay Continuous Base Polyiso:** Fasten a base layer of 2-inch flat polyisocyanurate rigid insulation (R-12.1) with 3-inch steel plates and heavy-duty roofing screws.
4. **Install Tapered Cricket System:** Lay tapered polyiso panels engineered to create a minimum 1/4-inch per foot slope toward the parapet scuppers, eliminating all flat dead spots.
5. **Install High-Density Cover Board:** Fasten a 1/2-inch high-density polyiso cover board (R-2.5, such as Dexcell or DensDeck Prime) over the tapered insulation. This provides high compressive strength (100+ psi) to resist hail strikes and foot traffic.
6. **Membrane Installation:**
   * **For EPDM:** Fully adhere 60-mil non-reinforced EPDM membrane using solvent-based bonding adhesive. Clean all seam overlaps with EPDM primer and seal with 3-inch double-sided factory-applied seam tape, followed by continuous lap sealant along all cut edges.
   * **For TPO:** Mechanically attach or fully adhere 60-mil polyester-reinforced TPO membrane. Fuse all 6-inch seam overlaps using a robotic hot-air welder (or handheld heat gun at 950 deg F), producing a continuous physical molecular weld.
7. **Parapet and Scupper Flashing:** Terminate the membrane a minimum of 8 inches up all vertical parapet walls. Flash the sheet-metal scupper sleeve with self-adhering target patches, extending the flange 4 inches past the wall into an exterior conductor head and 4-inch downspout.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is better for a Midwest home: EPDM or TPO?
In cold northern Midwest climates (Climate Zone 6A, such as Minnesota and Wisconsin), black EPDM is often preferred because its black rubber surface absorbs winter solar heat, melting snow and reducing ice accumulation. In mixed-humid zones (Climate Zone 4A/5A, such as St. Louis, Indianapolis, and Chicago), white TPO is popular for its high solar reflectance, reducing air conditioning costs during humid summers. For durability, TPO's hot-air welded seams are physically fused, whereas EPDM relies on adhesive tape.

### Can I install a single-ply flat roof directly over my old rolled roofing?
No. Laying a new membrane over wet, deteriorated asphalt or rolled roofing traps moisture inside the assembly, accelerating roof deck rot and causing fastener corrosion. Furthermore, trapped moisture boils in summer heat, creating steam blisters that rupture the new single-ply membrane. A full tear-off down to structural sheathing is always required.

### How do you clear ice that blocks flat roof scuppers in winter?
Never attack frozen roof scuppers with metal shovels, axes, or picks, which puncture single-ply membranes instantly. Commercial roofing contractors clear frozen scuppers using low-pressure steam machines. To prevent recurring freezing, install self-regulating 120V de-icing heating cables looped through the scupper sleeve and down the conductor head, as detailed in our [roof heat tape engineering guide](roof-heat-tape-deicing-cables.html).
