# Foundation Grading and French Drains: The 5% Slope Rule and Swale Geometries for Saturated Yards

> **Quick Summary:** In residential developments across the Midwest, heavy clay soils (such as glacial till and expansive smectite clays) dominate the landscape. When spring rains fall, water does not absorb quickly into dense clay; it pools on the surface and flows along the path of least resistance. In homes where soil has settled around the foundation, surface runoff flows backward toward the foundation stem wall. This water pools against the loose, porous soil of the original construction excavation (the clay bowl effect), generating massive hydrostatic head pressure that forces water through foundation cracks, cold joints, and floor seams.
>
> - IRC Section R401.3 mandates a minimum slope of 6 inches of fall within the first 10 feet away from the foundation (a 5% grade).
> - Gutter downspouts discharging within 3 feet of a foundation dump thousands of gallons of concentrated roof water directly into the foundation backfill during a 1-inch rainstorm.
> - Parabolic surface swales sloped at 1% to 2% are far more effective at moving high-volume surface runoff than underground pipe systems.
> - Deep subsurface French drains require washed 3/4-inch to 1.5-inch crushed river stone wrapped in non-woven geotextile filter fabric; using pea gravel or woven weed barrier fabric causes rapid silt clogging within 2 seasons.

## IRC Section R401.3 Surface Drainage Requirements

The International Residential Code (IRC) explicitly specifies surface grading parameters in Section R401.3:
* **The 5% Rule:** The ground immediately adjacent to foundation walls must be sloped downward away from the building at a slope of not less than 6 inches within the first 10 feet (5%).
* **Constrained Lot Exception:** Where physical site lines, walls, or lot boundaries prevent a 10-foot apron, the ground must slope away at a minimum 2% grade toward an engineered swale or drainage structure.
* **Impervious Surfaces:** Concrete sidewalks, driveways, and patios adjacent to the foundation must slope away from the building at not less than 2% (approximately 1/4 inch per foot).

```
             (Water drains to basement)                (Water drains away to swale)

                 Foundation Wall                           Foundation Wall

                     │  Settled Dirt                           │
                     ▼  (Pools water)                          ▼

```

When soil settles adjacent to the house, it creates a negative funnel. Homeowners often compound the problem by piling wood mulch against the foundation. Wood mulch does not create a grade; it acts as a giant sponge that retains moisture against the siding and concrete, attracting termites and promoting carpenter ant colonies, as outlined in our [pest barrier engineering guide](bug-prevention.html).

## French Drain Cross-Section and Hydraulic Mechanics

A French drain is not designed to handle high-velocity surface water; it is a subsurface groundwater interception tool. Installing a French drain improperly guarantees premature silt clogging.

```
                  CROSS-SECTION: ENGINEERED FRENCH DRAIN TRENCH

                     [ Surface Turf or River Rock ]
                     ┌────────────────────────────┐
                     │ Non-Woven Geotextile Wrap  │ <--- Heavy 4-6 oz Non-Woven Fabric
                     │ (Overlapped 12" at top)    │      (Allows water, blocks silt)
                     ├────────────────────────────┤
                     │                            │
                     │ Washed 3/4" Crushed Stone  │ <--- High-void gravel bed
                     │                            │
                     ├────────────────────────────┤
                     │ 4" Perforated PVC Pipe     │ <--- Holes oriented DOWN (4 & 8 o'clock)
                     │ (Smooth interior SDR-35)   │
                     ├────────────────────────────┤
                     │ 2" Washed Stone Bedding    │
                     └────────────────────────────┘
                     [ Solid Trench Base (1% Slope) ]
```

### Critical Installation Rules:
1. **Never Use Perforated Corrugated Black Pipe:** Thin corrugated black polyethylene tubing has interior ridges that trap silt and debris, flattening under wheel barrow loads. Use rigid 4-inch SDR-35 or Schedule 40 perforated PVC pipe with a smooth interior wall.
2. **Holes Oriented Downward:** Lay the pipe with the perforations facing downward (at the 4 o'clock and 8 o'clock positions). Groundwater rises into the trench from the bottom up; orienting holes downward allows water to enter at the lowest elevation and drain away immediately.
3. **Non-Woven Geotextile Only:** Line the entire trench with 4-to-6-ounce non-woven polypropylene geotextile fabric (such as Mirafi 140N). Never use black woven plastic weed barrier, which has tiny pore openings that blind over with silt within 12 months.
4. **Washed Angular Stone:** Use washed 3/4-inch to 1-inch clean angular crushed limestone or river rock. Never use "crushed run" or gravel with dust/fines, which compacts into concrete and blocks water flow.

## Step-by-Step Foundation Slope Correction Protocol

1. **Elevation Benchmark:** Drive wooden stakes at the foundation wall and 10 feet out. Use a string level or laser level to establish a reference plane.
2. **Excavation of Failed Organic Material:** Remove organic mulch, sod, and loose garden topsoil 3 to 5 feet out from the foundation down to solid native subsoil.
3. **Compact Clay Sub-Base:** Import dense clay-loam soil. Deposit in 2-inch lifts and compact using a vibrating plate compactor or hand tamper, shaping a firm 5% downward slope (6 inches of fall over 10 feet).
4. **Protective Membrane (Optional High-Risk Detail):** On houses with chronic basement seepage, lay a 6-foot wide sheet of 20-mil reinforced polyethylene or ethylene propylene diene monomer (EPDM) along the compacted clay apron, terminating 2 inches below siding into a reglet. This creates an impervious underground umbrella.
5. **Topsoil & Sod Dressing:** Cap the compacted clay with 2 to 3 inches of fertile topsoil and install sod to stabilize the bank against rain erosion.
6. **Bury Downspout Conductors:** Connect all gutter downspouts into solid-wall 4-inch PVC pipes. Trench the pipes away from the house at a minimum 1% slope, discharging into daylight on a downhill slope at least 15 to 20 feet from the foundation with a pop-up emitter or grated animal guard.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I just pile dirt against my foundation to fix the grade?
No. You must maintain a minimum 6-inch clearance between the top of the finished soil grade and the bottom edge of wood siding, brick veneer weep holes, or stucco. Piling dirt over siding creates a direct path for rot, mold, and subterranean termites. If you cannot raise the grade near the house without touching siding, you must excavate the soil farther out to create a shallow parabolic drainage swale.

### Where should French drain discharge pipes exit?
French drains must discharge to "daylight" on a lower portion of the property, into a municipal storm sewer (where permitted by local ordinance), or into an engineered underground dry well sized for soil percolation rates. Never discharge a drainage pipe across a public sidewalk or within 10 feet of a neighbor's property line.

### How far should downspout extensions carry water?
Downspouts should discharge a minimum of 10 to 15 feet away from the foundation wall. Discharging water 2 to 3 feet from the house dumps roof runoff directly into the uncompacted backfill trench surrounding your foundation, where it immediately sinks to your footing drain and overwhelms your sump pump.
