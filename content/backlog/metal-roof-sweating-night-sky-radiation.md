# Underside Metal Roof Sweating: Night-Sky Radiation Cooling, Thermal Shock, and High-Temp Underlayments

Across residential standing seam roofs, unvented post-frame pole barns, and architectural metal canopies throughout the Midwest, a baffling condensation mystery occurs every spring and autumn. On clear, calm nights with zero precipitation and moderate outdoor humidity, the underside of metal roof panels begins sweating profusely. 

By sunrise, thousands of gallons of water condense on the bottom face of the steel panels, raining down onto attic insulation, drywall ceilings, parked vehicles, and stored equipment.

Homeowners and builders invariably assume the roof has an invisible pinhole leak or that flashing is failing. However, when the roof is inspected the next afternoon, not a single leak can be found. 

The phenomenon is entirely thermodynamic: **night-sky radiation cooling**. 

Due to the fundamental laws of radiant heat transfer, high-emissivity metal roof panels radiate heat directly into the deep vacuum of space, sub-cooling the metal surface to temperatures **10 to 15 degrees Fahrenheit colder than the surrounding ambient air**. When moist air contacts this sub-cooled metal plate, condensation is instantaneous and severe.

## 1. Thermodynamic Physics of Stefan-Boltzmann Radiation Sub-Cooling

Heat transfer occurs via conduction, convection, and thermal radiation. While conduction and convection dominate building envelope analysis, night-sky radiation cooling is purely a radiative phenomenon governed by the **Stefan-Boltzmann Law**:

$$q_{\text{rad}} = \epsilon \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_{\text{roof}}^4 - T_{\text{sky}}^4)$$

Where:
* $q_{\text{rad}}$ is the net radiant heat flux from the roof surface (in $\text{BTU}/(\text{hr}\cdot\text{ft}^2)$ or $\text{W/m}^2$).
* $\epsilon$ is the thermal emissivity of the painted metal surface (typically $\epsilon \approx 0.85\text{ to }0.90$ for standard coil-coated Kynar 500 / PVDF finishes).
* $\sigma$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant ($5.670 \times 10^{-8}\text{ W}/(\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4)$).
* $T_{\text{roof}}$ is the absolute temperature of the metal roof panel.
* $T_{\text{sky}}$ is the effective radiative temperature of the clear night sky.

### The Phenomenon of the "Cold Sky Sink"
On a clear, cloudless, low-humidity night in the Midwest, the atmosphere is transparent to long-wave infrared radiation ($8\text{ to }14\text{ microns}$, the "atmospheric window"). The effective radiative temperature of the deep night sky ($T_{\text{sky}}$) is not the ambient air temperature; it can be **$-40^\circ\text{F}\text{ to }-60^\circ\text{F}$ ($-40^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }-51^\circ\text{C}$)**!

Because the metal roof panel has a high emissivity ($\epsilon \approx 0.85$) and very low thermal mass (standard 26-gauge steel is only $0.018\text{ inches}$ thick), it radiates heat outward into space far faster than convective outdoor air can warm it.

As a result, **the temperature of the metal roof panel drops 8 to 15 degrees Fahrenheit below the ambient air temperature**:

$$T_{\text{roof}} = T_{\text{ambient}} - \Delta T_{\text{sub-cooling}}$$

### Psychrometric Dew-Point Inversion
Consider a typical calm October night across the Midwest:
* Outdoor ambient air temperature is **$50^\circ\text{F}$**.
* Relative humidity is **$80\%$**, corresponding to a **dew point temperature of $44^\circ\text{F}$**.
* Standard convective physics suggests no condensation can occur, because the air is 6 degrees above its dew point.
* However, night-sky radiative cooling sub-cools the metal roof sheet down to **$36^\circ\text{F}$**.

Because the metal surface temperature ($36^\circ\text{F}$) is a full **8 degrees below the dew point of the surrounding air ($44^\circ\text{F}$)**, the air contacting both the top and the underside of the metal sheet reaches 100 percent saturation immediately. 

Moisture condenses into massive droplets on the underside of the panel. Because uninsulated steel has zero water absorption capacity, the droplets coalesce into continuous streams that run down the panel flutes and rain down into the building.

## 2. Why Conventional Roofing Practices and Aggregators Fail

Generic roofing blogs provide advice that exacerbates metal roof sweating:

1. **Relying on Standard Asphalt Felt (15# or 30# Tar Paper):** Many contractors install cheap organic asphalt felt under standing seam metal roofs. Organic felt breaks down and chars under high metal roof temperatures (which reach $160^\circ\text{F}\text{ to }190^\circ\text{F}$ in direct summer sun). More critically, saturated felt holds moisture against the bottom of the steel panel, accelerating galvanic corrosion and rust perforations from the underside out.
2. **Treating Unvented Pole Barns as "Leaking Roofs":** Post-frame pole barn owners often spend thousands of dollars caulking screw fasteners and ridge caps trying to stop "leaks." Fastener seals are almost never the problem. An uninsulated, unvented metal roof over a dirt or concrete floor has high internal humidity; night-sky radiation guarantees interior raining unless a thermal break or drip-stop membrane is installed.
3. **Using Standard Low-Temp Ice & Water Shields:** Standard residential peel-and-stick membranes use asphaltic adhesives with a softening point of only $180^\circ\text{F}$. Under dark metal panels in summer, the asphalt melts, oozing black goo into gutters and through soffit joints. High-temperature elastomeric membranes are mandatory.

## 3. Forensic Roofing Diagnostic Protocol

When evaluating a sweating metal roof or diagnosing phantom winter/spring leaks, follow this sequence.

| Diagnostic Step | Observed Condition | Forensic Root Failure Mode | Corrective Engineering Action |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **01** | Water drips only on clear, calm nights with zero rain; stops by noon | Night-sky radiation sub-cooling the metal panel below ambient dew point | Install thermal break insulation beneath panels or add ventilation. |
| **02** | Underside of uninsulated pole barn steel sheets dripping water onto floor | Interior air humidity condensing directly on sub-cooled metal skin | Apply closed-cell spray foam to steel, or install DripStop fleece panels. |
| **03** | Peel-and-stick membrane under metal panels has liquefied and oozed | Standard asphalt membrane used; failed under high operating temperatures | Strip failed membrane; install high-temp self-adhering underlayment ($> 250^\circ\text{F}$). |
| **04** | Standing seam roof over solid OSB deck has wet sheathing | Warm, moist indoor air leaking into cold attic space through ceiling bypasses | Air-seal attic floor bypasses; verify soffit-to-ridge net free ventilation. |
| **05** | Fastener rubber washers cracked, dried out, and missing | UV and thermal shock degradation of low-grade neoprene screws | Replace with heavy-duty EPDM cupped-head stainless steel wood screws. |

### Installation Standard (SMACNA & ASTM D1970 Compliance)
1. **High-Temperature Self-Adhering Membrane Standard:**
   * On all residential standing seam and architectural metal roof assemblies installed over solid wood decking, install a continuous high-temperature self-adhering waterproofing membrane conforming to **ASTM D1970** (such as Grace Ultra, Carlisle WIP 300HT, or Polyglass Polystick XFR).
   * **Temperature Resistance Specification:** The membrane must be formulated with synthetic butyl or specialized SBS-modified rubberized asphalt rated for continuous operating temperatures of **at least $240^\circ\text{F}\text{ to }260^\circ\text{F}$ ($115^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }127^\circ\text{C}$)** without softening or adhesive migration.
   * The membrane serves a dual purpose: it acts as a secondary waterproof barrier, and it forms a direct thermal decoupling buffer between the cold steel and the wooden roof sheathing.
2. **Unvented Post-Frame Pole Barn Specification:**
   * For agricultural or post-frame buildings lacking a solid wood deck (where metal panels are fastened directly across dimensional wood or steel purlins):
     * *Factory Anti-Condensation Fleece:* Specify metal panels manufactured with factory-applied non-woven polyester anti-condensation fleece bonded to the underside (such as DripStop). The fleece matrix acts as a sponge, holding up to **25 gallons of water per 1,000 square feet** during night-sky sub-cooling, and releasing it harmlessly via evaporation as daytime sun warms the building.
     * *Closed-Cell Spray Foam Insulation:* Alternatively, apply a minimum **2-inch continuous layer of 2.0-lb closed-cell polyurethane spray foam (R-14)** directly to the underside of the metal roof panels. The closed-cell foam isolates the cold metal from interior air, permanently eliminating the condensation surface.
3. **Ventilation Geometry:**
   * On structures with cold attics, maintain a balanced cross-ventilation ratio of **1:150** (IRC Section R806.2). Ensure that 50 percent of the ventilation is provided by continuous perforated soffit intake vents, and 50 percent is exhausted through continuous baffled ridge vents. Continuous convective airflow purges moisture before night-sky sub-cooling can cause condensation.

## 6. Building Code Citations and Metal Roofing Standards

* **International Residential Code (IRC) Section R905.10:** Metal roof shingles and panels; mandates installation in accordance with manufacturer specifications and approved underlayment standards.
* **ASTM D1970 / D1970M:** Standard Specification for Self-Adhering Polymer Modified Bituminous Sheet Materials Used as Steep Roofing Underlayment for Ice Dam Protection (including High-Temperature provisions).
* **SMACNA Architectural Sheet Metal Manual (7th Edition):** Chapter 6 (Roofing and Flashing), establishing underlayment separation rules and thermal expansion tolerances for architectural sheet metal.
* **UL 2218:** Standard for Impact Resistance of Prepared Roof Covering Materials (Class 4 Hail Resistance).
