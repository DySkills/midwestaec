# Spray Foam Roof Deck Fire Codes: Thermal Barriers (1/2" Gypsum) vs DC315 Intumescent Paint

Unvented conditioned attics created by applying spray polyurethane foam (SPF) directly to the underside of the structural roof deck have become a gold standard for energy efficiency across the Midwest. By moving the building's thermal envelope from the attic floor to the roofline, unvented attics bring HVAC ductwork and furnaces inside conditioned space, reducing heating and cooling loads by 15 to 25 percent.

However, spray polyurethane foam is an organic plastic material derived from petrochemicals. When exposed to high heat or direct flame, exposed polyurethane undergoes rapid pyrolysis, releasing combustible hydrocarbon gases and toxic hydrogen cyanide ($HCN$) smoke. 

To protect life safety, building codes strictly regulate the installation of spray foam in residential structures.

Building inspectors throughout Climate Zones 4A, 5A, and 6A frequently fail unvented attic insulation jobs because the foam is left raw and exposed. Homeowners and contractors face intense confusion regarding the difference between a **15-minute Thermal Barrier**, an **Ignition Barrier**, and an **Approved Alternative Assembly tested under NFPA 286** (such as DC315 intumescent coating).

## 1. Fire Combustion Chemistry of Polyurethane Foam

Polyurethane foam is formed through the exothermic chemical reaction of polymeric diphenylmethane diisocyanate (MDI) with a blended polyol resin. 

Although modern formulations incorporate halogenated or phosphorus-based flame retardants to achieve an ASTM E84 Class A flame spread index ($FSI \le 25$, $SDI \le 450$), the bulk polymer remains combustible.

### Pyrolysis and Flashover Dynamics
When exposed to thermal radiation exceeding **$300^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }400^\circ\text{C}$ ($572^\circ\text{F}\text{ to }752^\circ\text{F}$)**, the urethane chemical bonds break down in a process called **pyrolysis**. 

Pyrolysis vaporizes the foam, generating volatile flammable gases (methane, ethylene, carbon monoxide, and isocyanate vapors). In an enclosed attic space, these combustible gases accumulate near the roof ridge. If an open flame or electrical spark ignites this gas cloud, the attic can experience catastrophic **flashover** in less than 90 seconds.

### The Thermal Barrier Standard (IRC Section R316.4)
To prevent flashover and provide building occupants sufficient time to evacuate, the International Residential Code establishes a universal baseline:

> **IRC Section R316.4:** Except where specifically approved, all spray-applied foam plastic insulation shall be separated from the interior of a building by an approved **thermal barrier** of not less than **1/2-inch ($12.7\text{ mm}$) gypsum board**, or an approved material that limits the average temperature rise of the unexposed surface to not more than $250^\circ\text{F}$ ($139^\circ\text{C}$) after **15 minutes** of fire exposure in accordance with the standard time-temperature curve of ASTM E119.

Hanging 1/2-inch gypsum drywall across the sloped, angled underside of roof rafters, around collar ties, and into narrow eave cavities is structurally complex, labor-intensive, and extremely expensive ($3.50\text{ to }5.50\text{ per sq ft}$).

## 2. Code Exceptions: Attics Used Solely for Storage vs Mechanicals

The building code provides two critical pathways that allow homeowners and contractors to avoid installing heavy 1/2-inch drywall in attics:

```
            ATTIC USE AND APPLICABLE FIRE PROTECTION PATHWAY
            
     IS THE ATTIC HABITABLE LIVING SPACE?
     |
     +---> YES: MUST have a 1/2" Gypsum Board Thermal Barrier!
     |          (Intumescent paint alone is NOT permitted in living areas!)
     |
     +---> NO: Is the attic entered ONLY for service of utilities?
           |
           +---> UNVENTED ATTIC WITH HVAC / WATER HEATER:
           |     Must have EITHER:
           |     1) DC315 Intumescent Coating (Tested per NFPA 286 / AC377)
           |     2) An Appendix X Ignition-Resistant Foam Formulation
           |
           +---> TRADITIONAL ATTIC WITH STORAGE ACCESS:
                 May use an IRC R316.5.3 "Ignition Barrier":
                 * 1-1/2" Mineral wool or fiberglass batts
                 * 1/4" Plywood or wood structural panel
                 * 3/8" Particleboard or gypsum board
```

### The NFPA 286 / ICC-ES AC377 Appendix X Standard
The most common and cost-effective method to satisfy the fire code in conditioned attics containing mechanical equipment (furnaces, air handlers, water heaters) is applying a certified intumescent paint under **IRC Section R316.6 (Specific Approval)**.

To receive an ICC-ES evaluation report, the specific brand of spray foam (e.g., Carlisle, Demilec, Johns Manville) must be tested in combination with a specific intumescent coating (e.g., International Fireproof Technology Inc. **DC315**) inside a full-scale room apparatus under **NFPA 286** or **ICC-ES AC377 Appendix X**.

The assembly is subjected to a severe $300\text{-kW}$ gas burner flame for 15 minutes. To pass, the assembly must prevent flashover, limit smoke production ($< 1,000\text{ m}^2$), and restrict flames from spreading to the extremities of the test room.

## 3. How Intumescent Coatings Work: The Chemistry of DC315

Intumescent paints such as DC315 are water-based, high-solids fire-retardant latex coatings formulated with three active chemical components:

1. **Inorganic Acid Catalyst:** Ammonium polyphosphate (APP).
2. **Carbon-Rich Carbonific Agent:** Pentaerythritol (PER).
3. **Blowing Agent:** Melamine.

```
                  INTUMESCENT FOAM CHAR SHIELD ACTIVATION
                  
   Room Temperature (Normal Attic)          Fire Exposure (> 400°F)
   +=============================+          +=============================+
   | DC315 Paint Layer           |          | EXPANDED CELLULAR CHAR      |
   | (18 to 22 Wet Mils Applied) |          | (Expands 50 Times in Depth!)|
   | Looks like smooth latex!    |          |                             |
   +-----------------------------+          | Non-combustible, dense      |
   | Spray Polyurethane Foam     | =======> | carbonaceous ceramic crust! |
   | Unvented Roof Deck Assembly |          |                             |
   |                             |          | Blocks 95% of flame heat;   |
   |                             |          | keeps foam below pyrolysis! |
   +=============================+          +=============================+
```

When exposed to heat exceeding **$400^\circ\text{F}$ ($204^\circ\text{C}$)**:
* The ammonium polyphosphate decomposes, releasing phosphoric acid.
* The acid reacts with the pentaerythritol, initiating carbonization.
* Simultaneously, the melamine blowing agent vaporizes, releasing non-flammable nitrogen gas.
* The gas bubbles puff the soft carbonaceous liquid into a thick, black, non-combustible ceramic foam char that expands to **50 times the original thickness of the paint**.

This insulating char layer shields the underlying polyurethane foam from heat, keeping the foam core temperature below its pyrolysis ignition point for the duration of the test.

## 4. Forensic Field Inspection Protocol

When inspecting an unvented spray foam attic for fire code compliance, apply this verification checklist.

| Inspection Step | Inspection Item | Building Code Standard | Pass / Fail Criteria |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **01** | Verify coating manufacturer & ICC-ES Report | Must have active ICC-ES report (e.g., ESR-3702 for DC315) matching foam brand | PASS: Coating approved with specific foam manufacturer. FAIL: Unlisted generic paint. |
| **02** | Measure Wet Film Thickness (WFT) during application | Measured using a notched stainless steel wet-film comb | PASS: Meets specified thickness (14 to 22 wet mils). FAIL: Thin "fog coat" (<10 mils). |
| **03** | Measure Dry Film Thickness (DFT) on cured coating | Measured using an ultrasonic or microscopic core cutter | PASS: Meets dry mil specification (9 to 15 dry mils). FAIL: Uneven, patchy coverage. |
| **04** | Verify foam surface texture before coating | Must have a smooth, stable surface profile | PASS: Sound foam skin. FAIL: Flaking, crumbling foam or wet foam sprayed off-ratio. |
| **05** | Inspect mechanical equipment clearances | Minimum clearance between coating and gas flues | PASS: 6" clearance from Category I metal flues; 0" for Category IV direct-vent PVC. |

```
     |                                                             |
     |   [WET-FILM COMB VERIFICATION]                              |

     |   rafter sides, eave corners, and rim joist intersections!  |
     +=============================================================+
```

### Installation Standard (ICC-ES ESR-3702 & NFPA 286)
1. **Surface Preparation:**
   * Allow the spray polyurethane foam to fully cure and off-gas for at least 24 hours before applying the intumescent coating.
   * Verify the foam was sprayed on-ratio (uniform density and cell structure). Remove any loose, friable, or crumbling foam. The surface must be clean, dry, and free of oil, sawdust, or silicone overspray.
2. **Application Equipment and Thickness Standard:**
   * Apply DC315 using an industrial airless sprayer capable of delivering at least 1.0 gallon per minute at **2,500 to 3,000 PSI** (such as a Graco Ultra Max II 795 or larger). Use a reversible spray tip with an orifice diameter of **0.027 to 0.031 inches**.
   * **Mil Thickness Specification:** Apply the coating in a single, continuous, uniform cross-hatch pass at the exact mil thickness required by the foam manufacturer's ICC-ES evaluation report:
     * *Open-Cell Foam (0.5 lb density):* Typically requires **18 to 22 wet mils (WFT)**, curing to approximately **12 to 14 dry mils (DFT)**, at a coverage rate of roughly 75 to 85 square feet per gallon.
     * *Closed-Cell Foam (2.0 lb density):* Typically requires **14 to 18 wet mils (WFT)**, curing to approximately **9 to 12 dry mils (DFT)**, at a coverage rate of roughly 90 to 110 square feet per gallon.
3. **Quality Control Verification:**
   * The applicator must carry a certified notched stainless steel wet-film thickness gauge (ASTM D4414). Measure wet film thickness every 100 square feet, paying special attention to rafter edges, pipe penetrations, and truss plates.
   * Affix a permanent jobsite certification sticker inside the attic access hatch listing the foam brand, the intumescent product name, the ICC-ES evaluation report number, the date applied, and the verified wet mil thickness.

## 7. Building Code Citations and Fire Standards

* **International Residential Code (IRC) Section R316.4:** Thermal barrier; mandates separation of foam plastics from building interiors by 1/2-inch gypsum board or equivalent 15-minute thermal barrier.
* **IRC Section R316.5.3:** Attics and crawlspaces; prescribes ignition barrier alternatives where entry is made only for service of utilities.
* **IRC Section R316.6:** Specific approval; permits foam plastic without a thermal barrier based on large-scale tests such as NFPA 286.
* **NFPA 286:** Standard Methods of Fire Tests for Evaluating Contribution of Wall and Ceiling Interior Finish to Room Fire Growth.
* **ICC-ES AC377 (Appendix X):** Acceptance Criteria for Spray-Applied Foam Plastic Insulation, defining the specific testing protocol for attic unvented assemblies.
