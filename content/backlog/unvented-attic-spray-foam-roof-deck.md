# Unvented Conditioned Attics: Open-Cell vs. Closed-Cell Spray Foam on Roof Decks

> **Quick Summary:** In modern home construction and deep energy retrofits across the Midwest, unvented conditioned attics (also called "hot roofs") have become a premier strategy to optimize building efficiency. By applying spray polyurethane foam directly to the underside of the roof sheathing and sealing all soffit, gable, and ridge vents, the entire attic volume is brought inside the conditioned building envelope. This eliminates summer ductwork heat gain (where attic air hits 130 deg F) and winter ice dams. However, installing spray foam on roof decks improperly in Climate Zones 5A and 6A causes severe sheathing rot and roof deck failure.
>
> - IRC Section R806.5 strictly governs unvented attic assemblies, mandating specific air-impermeable insulation criteria to prevent interior moisture from condensing against cold roof sheathing.
> - Open-cell spray foam (0.5 lb density, R-3.8/inch) is vapor-permeable (10+ perms); in Climate Zones 5 and 6, code requires a continuous Class II vapor retarder (such as vapor-retarding primer) applied over open-cell foam.
> - Closed-cell spray foam (2.0 lb density, R-6.8/inch) acts as its own vapor barrier (under 0.8 perms at 2 inches), provides structural racking strength, and resists liquid water entry.
> - Spray foam on the underside of roof sheathing only increases asphalt shingle temperatures by 2 to 5 deg F, completely dispelling the myth that spray foam "bakes shingles and voids warranties."

## IRC Section R806.5 Unvented Attic Hygrothermal Physics

In a conventional vented attic, winter moisture that leaks up from the living space is diluted by outdoor air circulating from soffits to the ridge. In an unvented conditioned attic, there is zero exterior ventilation air.

In winter, warm indoor air (at 70 deg F and 40% RH) carries moisture with a dew point of approximately 45 deg F. When outdoor temperatures drop to 0 deg F, the wood roof sheathing cools to near 0 deg F. If this indoor moisture touches the cold wood sheathing, it condenses into liquid water and freezes into ice lenses, rotting the roof deck within 2 to 3 winters.

IRC Section R806.5 mandates that the insulation directly contacting the underside of the roof sheathing must be air-impermeable. Furthermore, in Climate Zones 5, 6, 7, and 8, if open-cell foam is used, a Class II vapor retarder coating (0.1 to 1.0 perm rating) must be applied directly to the interior surface of the foam, or closed-cell foam must be used.

## The Shingle Temperature Myth

A persistent myth among homeowners and older contractors is that spray foam under the roof deck "cooks" asphalt shingles, causing premature curling, blistering, and voiding the manufacturer's warranty.

Extensive empirical research conducted by the Florida Solar Energy Center (FSEC) and Building Science Corporation (BSC) monitored shingle temperatures across identical houses with vented vs. unvented attics:
* In peak midsummer sun, asphalt shingles on a vented attic reach 155 to 165 deg F.
* Shingles on an unvented spray-foamed roof deck reach 158 to 168 deg F.
* The actual temperature increase is only **2 to 5 deg F**.

Color and solar orientation drive shingle temperature far more than attic insulation; changing shingle color from white to black increases shingle temperature by 20 to 25 deg F. Major shingle manufacturers (including GAF, Owens Corning, and CertainTeed) explicitly permit unvented conditioned attic assemblies beneath their roofing materials without voiding warranties, provided installation conforms to IRC R806.5.

## Step-by-Step Unvented Attic Spray Foam Retrofit Protocol

1. **Seal All Exterior Attic Vents:** Permanently seal all soffit intake vents, gable louvers, and ridge vents. Fasten 1/2-inch exterior plywood or rigid foam over the openings from the interior and can-foam the perimeter airtight.
2. **Inspect and Repair Roof Sheathing:** Ensure roof sheathing is 100% dry (moisture content under 15% measured with a pin meter). Replace any rotted, delaminated, or sagging plywood or OSB before applying foam.
3. **HVAC Combustion Appliance Safety Check:** Before sealing the attic, inspect all heating equipment. Atmospheric gas water heaters or furnaces with Category I metal flues CANNOT remain in an unvented attic because they draw combustion air from the attic space, causing deadly backdrafting. All appliances must be direct-vent sealed-combustion units (Category IV PVC flue) drawing dedicated outdoor air.
4. **Apply Closed-Cell Base Lift (Flash and Batt or Full Foam):**
   * **Hybrid "Flash and Batt":** Spray a minimum of 2 inches of 2.0 lb closed-cell spray foam (R-14) directly onto the underside of the roof sheathing and rafters. This establishes the continuous Class II vapor barrier and air barrier. Fill the remainder of the rafter bay with friction-fit stone wool batts (Rockwool Comfortbatt) to reach total code R-value (R-49 to R-60).
   * **Full Closed-Cell System:** Spray 7 to 8 inches of 2.0 lb closed-cell foam directly to the deck to satisfy the full prescriptive R-49 ceiling requirement.
5. **Rafter Tail and Top Plate Detailing:** Extend the spray foam down over the exterior wall top plates to tie directly into the exterior wall air barrier, eliminating the notorious cold rim joist air bypass.
6. **Code-Mandated Ignition Barrier Application:** Per IRC Section R316.5.3, if the attic is used for service of HVAC equipment or storage, all exposed spray foam must be protected with an approved ignition barrier:
   * 1-1/2-inch thick mineral wool batts, or
   * Approved intumescent paint coating (such as DC315) applied at specified wet-film thickness (typically 14 to 18 mils).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I spray foam directly onto the underside of roof sheathing if I have a roof leak?
No. If the roof shingles or flashings leak, water will penetrate the sheathing. With closed-cell foam, the foam rejects water, trapping it between the waterproof foam and the waterproof shingles. The wood sheathing cannot dry in either direction and will rot rapidly without showing an interior drip. The roof shingles and underlayment must be in sound condition before spray foam is applied.

### Does an unvented attic require conditioned air supply?
Yes. IRC Section R806.5 requires unvented attics to be conditioned. In the Midwest, you must provide conditioned air to the attic space at a rate of not less than 50 CFM per 1,000 sq ft of attic floor area from the central HVAC system, or install a dedicated supply duct and return air path to circulate air and control summer humidity.

### What is the difference between open-cell and closed-cell spray foam in freezing weather?
Open-cell foam consists of tiny ruptured cells filled with air; it is soft, flexible, and vapor-permeable. Closed-cell foam consists of sealed microscopic bubbles filled with an eco-friendly fluorocarbon blowing agent (such as Solstice HFO); it cures into a dense, rock-hard plastic matrix that is 100% waterproof, airtight, and vapor-impermeable. In Climate Zones 5 and 6, closed-cell foam is vastly superior for roof decks because it eliminates the risk of winter vapor condensation.
