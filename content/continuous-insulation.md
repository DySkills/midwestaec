# Continuous Exterior Insulation: Why R-5 Outboard Beats R-20 Cavity in Freeze-Thaw Climates

> **Executive Summary:** Wood framing studs account for roughly 25% of a wall's surface area. Because wood conducts heat roughly 4 times faster than fiberglass, an "R-20" stud cavity wall has a real-world effective rating of barely R-15. Adding just 1 to 2 inches of continuous outboard rigid foam or mineral wool breaks this thermal bridge, warms the structural sheathing, and prevents internal condensation during Midwest freeze/thaw cycles.

## 1. The Myth of the "R-20" Cavity Wall
*Explain thermal bridging. Every 16 inches or 24 inches on-center, a 2x6 stud creates a thermal bypass from heated drywall directly to cold sheathing. In January when it is 0°F outside in Chicago or Des Moines, the temperature of exterior OSB behind cavity-only insulation drops to 28°F, creating a condensation plane.*

## 2. Dew Point Control: The Physics of Warming the Sheathing
*Explain how continuous exterior insulation keeps the sheathing above the indoor dew point (typically 45°F–50°F at 70°F / 40% RH indoors). When the sheathing stays warm, moisture vapor migrating through the wall cannot condense into liquid water, eliminating rot risk.*

## 4. The Siding Replacement Opportunity
*Detail why the best time to install continuous insulation is during a re-siding project. When old vinyl or wood siding is stripped off, adding 1.5 inches of continuous insulation adds only $1.20 to $2.00/sq.ft in material cost while cutting whole-home heating bills by 20% permanently.*
