# Crawlspace Encapsulation vs. Open Venting in the Midwest: Mold, Odors & Heating Costs

> **Executive Summary:** For decades, building codes mandated open vents in crawlspaces under the flawed theory that outdoor air would "dry out" moisture. In the Midwest, the exact opposite happens. In July and August, 90°F outdoor air carrying 80% relative humidity enters the cool 65°F crawlspace. When warm humid air hits cold surfaces, the relative humidity spikes past 100%, causing water to condense on floor joists, rot wood framing, and breed black mold that migrates upstairs via the stack effect.

## 1. The Physics of the Midwest Summer Condensation Trap
*Explain psychrometric charts and relative humidity. Warm air holds more moisture than cold air. When outdoor humid summer air enters an open-vented crawlspace, it cools down. For every 20°F drop in temperature, relative humidity doubles. A crawlspace with open vents is an active mold incubator throughout the Midwest summer.*

## 2. The Winter Penalty: Freezing Floors & Exploding Heating Bills
*In winter, open crawlspace vents allow sub-zero air directly under your living room and kitchen floors. Fiberglas batts stuffed between floor joists sag, absorb ground moisture, and become useless as cold drafts chill heating ducts running through the unconditioned space.*

## 3. Specification Standards: Open Venting vs. Closed Encapsulation

| Metric | Open Vented Crawlspace | Sealed Conditioned Encapsulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Summer Relative Humidity** | 75% – 95% (High Mold Risk) | 45% – 55% (Maintained by Dehumidifier) |
| **Ground Vapor Retarder** | Thin 6-mil poly (Torn, unsealed) | 12-mil to 20-mil Reinforced Poly (Class A) |
| **Stem Wall Insulation** | None (Cold foundation concrete) | 2" Continuous EPS or Closed-Cell Spray Foam (R-10) |
| **Annual Heating/Cooling Savings** | 0% (Baseline) | **15% – 25% Reduction** |

## 4. The 4 Steps to a Code-Compliant Midwest Encapsulation (IRC R408.3)
1. **Seal All Perimeter Foundation Vents:** Permanently seal vents with rigid foam and polyurethane sealant.
2. **Heavy-Duty Ground Vapor Barrier:** Install minimum 12-mil reinforced poly across 100% of the dirt floor, overlapping seams 12 inches and taping with commercial mastic tape. Run the barrier 12 inches up foundation stem walls.
3. **Insulate Foundation Walls & Rim Joists:** Apply R-10 continuous insulation to the interior of foundation walls and rim joists (keeping a 3-inch termite inspection gap at the top).
4. **Dedicated Dehumidification:** Install an ENERGY STAR commercial crawlspace dehumidifier (e.g., Santa Fe or AprilAire) draining automatically to daylight or a sump pit.

## 5. Cost Breakdown & What to Look for in a Contractor
*Typical costs: $5,000 to $12,000 depending on crawlspace square footage and debris removal. Contractor checklist: Ensure the company installs a true commercial dehumidifier with digital humidistat, not a cheap residential basement unit that burns out in 18 months.*
