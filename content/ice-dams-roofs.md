# Why Do Midwest Roofs Get Ice Dams? The Real Fix (And Why Heat Cables Don't Work)

> **Executive Summary:** Every January and February across the Upper Midwest, heavy snowstorms are followed by roof ice dams—massive ridges of solid ice along eaves that back up under shingles and leak water into interior walls and ceilings. Hardware stores make millions selling zigzag electric heat cables, but building science proves heat cables merely melt small channels while increasing your electric bill. Ice dams are not a roof problem; they are an attic air-leakage and insulation problem.

## 1. The Physics of an Ice Dam
*Explain the 3-step thermal cycle:*
1. *Heat escapes from the living space into the attic via air bypasses (can lights, bath fans, attic hatch, unsealed wall plates).*
2. *This escaped heat warms the underside of the roof deck above 32°F, melting the bottom layer of snow on the roof.*
3. *Meltwater trickles down the roof slope under the snow blanket until it reaches the cold eave/overhang (which extends past the heated exterior wall and sits below 32°F). The water instantly refreezes, forming an ice dam that grows thicker every day.*

## 2. Why Heat Cables and Roof Shoveling Are Band-Aids
*Explain why zigzag electric heat cables fail: They draw 5 to 8 watts per foot 24/7, creating miniature icicles and ice tunnels while leaving the root cause unaddressed. Furthermore, chopping at ice with axes or hammers damages asphalt shingles and punctures underlayment.*

## 3. Specification Standards: Cold Roof Ventilation vs. Ceiling Plane Seal

| Building Science Standard | Code Reference | Recommended Specification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ceiling Plane Airtightness** | ASTM E779 / IECC R402.4 | 100% of ceiling penetrations sealed with foam/mastic before insulating |
| **Attic Thermal Resistance** | IECC Climate Zone 5 | **R-60 Blown-in Cellulose or Mineral Wool (18 to 20 inches)** |
| **Net Free Vent Area (NFVA)** | IRC Section R806.2 | 1:150 ratio (or 1:300 if 50% vent area is at ridge and vapor retarder present) |
| **Eave Ice & Water Shield** | IRC Section R905.1.2 | Self-adhered modified bitumen membrane extending 24" inside heated wall line |

## 4. The 3-Step Permanent Solution
1. **Seal Every Attic Thermal Bypass:** Move existing insulation aside and foam-seal chimney chases, plumbing stacks, top plates, and recessed light cans.
2. **Install Rigid Soffit Vent Baffles:** Ensure every rafter bay has an uninterrupted plastic or cardboard baffle preventing blown insulation from blocking soffit intake airflow.
3. **Blow R-60 Dense-Pack Cellulose:** Ensure uniform thermal depth across the entire ceiling plane, keeping the roof deck sub-freezing.

## 5. Contractor Checklist for Ice Dam Prevention
*What to demand from a roofing and insulation contractor: Verifying soffit intake airflow with smoke pencils and installing full 6-foot ice and water shield membranes on all eaves during roof replacement.*
