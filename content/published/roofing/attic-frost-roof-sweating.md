# Frost in the Attic: Why Midwest Roof Sheathing Sweats and Frosts in Winter, and How to Stop It

> **Quick Summary:** Frost on the underside of the roof means warm, moist air from the house is reaching the cold roof deck and freezing on it. The moisture comes from showers, cooking and humidifiers, and it gets into the attic through gaps in the ceiling. More vents and more insulation usually don't fix it. Sealing the ceiling, venting fans outside and keeping winter humidity down do.
>
> - Moisture reaches the attic mostly by air leaks, not through the drywall.
> - Seal the ceiling before adding anything else.
> - Vent every bath and kitchen fan all the way outdoors.
> - Keep indoor humidity around 30 to 40 percent in winter.

---

You climb into the attic in January to check something and find the underside of the roof covered in white frost, with little icicles hanging from every nail. It looks dramatic, and it is a warning: the attic is getting a steady supply of moist air from the house.

## Where Is the Moisture Coming From?

Everyday life puts a lot of water into the air: showers, cooking, breathing, drying clothes and, in many Midwest homes, a whole-house humidifier. The Minnesota Department of Commerce's *Home Energy Guide* (July 2025) explains what happens when that air escapes into the attic: the vapor condenses, freezes and builds up in the insulation and on the roof deck.

How does it get up there?
Mostly through air leaks. Building Science Corporation's attic ventilation digest (Lstiburek, BSD-102) calls stopping air movement, "particularly exfiltrating air in cold climates," the main strategy, and treats vapor moving through the drywall as secondary. Warm air rises, so in winter it pushes up through any gap in the ceiling: recessed lights, plumbing and wiring holes, the hatch, and soffits over cabinets.

Why does the frost start on the nails?
They're the coldest thing in the attic, since they conduct cold from the shingles straight through the deck. Minneapolis home inspector Reuben Saltzman notes frost shows up there first and then spreads across the sheathing.

## Why More Vents Don't Fix It

It seems logical that more ventilation would carry the moisture away. But BSD-102 says plainly that a vented attic needs an air barrier at the ceiling, and Building Science Corporation's ice dam digest (BSD-135) calls trying to ventilate away significant air leakage "very difficult and bad practise."

Adding vents to a leaky ceiling just gives the leaking air an easier path. The Minnesota *Home Energy Guide* also advises against adding roof vents as a fix.

Powered attic fans are worse. University of Minnesota Extension doesn't recommend them in Minnesota at all, since they can pull more air out of the house and into the attic.

## Why More Insulation Doesn't Fix It Either

Insulation slows heat, not air. Piling more insulation on an unsealed ceiling keeps the roof deck colder while the moist air keeps coming through, so the frost can actually get heavier. Saltzman makes the same point from his inspections.

Seal first, then insulate.

## How Do I Stop It?

Where should I seal?
Start with the biggest openings: the attic hatch, plumbing and flue chases, dropped soffits over kitchens and bathrooms, and recessed lights that aren't airtight-rated. Then the top plates of interior walls and the small wiring holes.

&rarr; [Attic Air Sealing and Baffles: A Step-by-Step Guide](attic-air-sealing-baffles.html)

Are the fans vented outside?
Check every bath and kitchen fan. Each duct should run to its own exterior vent cap, not end in the attic or point at a soffit vent, and it should be insulated where it runs through the attic so the moisture doesn't condense inside the duct. The *Home Energy Guide* suggests running bath fans at least 15 minutes after a shower.

Is the humidity too high?
The *Home Energy Guide* recommends 30 to 40 percent relative humidity in the heating season. Turn down or off a whole-house humidifier first. If the inside of your windows fogs on cold mornings, the attic is getting the same moist air.

## When the Frost Melts

The day a thaw arrives, all that frost turns to water at once, and it comes through the ceiling at light fixtures and fans. That's often when people first call a roofer, even though the roof is fine.

If the sheathing is dark, stained or soft in places, have it checked. Wood that stays wet for long periods can decay, and a roofer or inspector can tell you if any of it needs replacing.
