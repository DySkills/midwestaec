# Adding Continuous Insulation When Replacing Your Roof: Over-Sheathing Cathedral Ceilings

> **Executive Summary:** In homes with cathedral ceilings, finished third-floor attics, or mid-century post-and-beam designs, there is zero room between the rafters to achieve modern R-49 or R-60 insulation codes. Tearing down interior drywall, historic lath-and-plaster, or tongue-and-groove wood ceilings to insulate is messy and cost-prohibitive. The single best time to fix this is when your roof needs replacement: installing continuous rigid insulation *above* the existing structural roof deck before laying new shingles.

## 1. The Cathedral Ceiling Dilemma
*Explain why cathedral ceilings in older Midwest homes are notorious for cold drafts in winter, stifling heat in summer, and persistent ice dams. Typical 2x6 or 2x8 rafters only hold R-19 to R-25 cavity insulation—less than half of modern building code.*

## 2. The Over-Sheathing ("Chainsaw Retrofit") Concept
*Explain the building science of the "Hot Roof" or over-deck continuous insulation assembly. By placing continuous rigid insulation boards (polyisocyanurate or high-density stone wool) directly on top of the original roof deck, the entire roof framing is brought inside the conditioned envelope, eliminating thermal bridging.*

## 3. Specification Standards: Above-Deck Rigid Foam Assemblies

| Layer (Bottom to Top) | Material Specification | Building Science Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Existing Roof Deck** | 1/2" Plywood, OSB, or Historic 1x Plank Deck | Structural substrate |
| **2. Air & Vapor Barrier** | Self-Adhered SBS Modified Bitumen Membrane | 100% airtight vapor seal stopping interior moisture |
| **3. Continuous Insulation** | **2 Layers of 2" Staggered Foil-Faced Polyiso (R-24)** | Eliminates thermal bridging; staggered joints prevent air leaks |
| **4. Ventilation Layer (Optional)** | 2x4 Sleepers / Furring Strips running vertically | Creates 1.5" ventilated cold-roof air channel under shingles |
| **5. Top Sheathing Deck** | 5/8" CDX Plywood fastened with long timber screws | Structural nailing base for new roofing |
| **6. Finished Roofing** | Architectural Asphalt Shingles or Standing Seam Metal | Weather barrier |

## 4. Dew Point Math: Avoiding Condensation in Climate Zone 5
*Explain IRC Section R806.5 requirements. In Climate Zone 5, at least R-20 of rigid insulation must be placed above the roof deck if cavity insulation is also used underneath, ensuring the condensing surface stays above dew point during sub-zero January nights.*

## 5. Contractor Checklist for Over-Deck Insulation
*Fascia and gutter detailing: Extending rafter tails or installing custom sub-fascia to accommodate the extra 4 to 5 inches of roof thickness. Specifying engineered structural screws (e.g., FastenMaster HeadLOK) rated for wind uplift.*
