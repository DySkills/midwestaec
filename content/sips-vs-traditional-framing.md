# SIPs (Structural Insulated Panels) in Zone 5: Total Cost vs. Traditional Stick Framing

> **Executive Summary:** Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs)—composed of a solid core of rigid foam insulation sandwiched between two structural sheets of oriented strand board (OSB)—are widely praised as the fastest route to an airtight, high-performance building envelope in cold climates. However, builders often experience sticker shock when looking only at raw material invoices. When accounting for reduced jobsite framing labor, downsized HVAC requirements, and zero-defect blower door testing, SIPs offer a compelling total-cost equation in Climate Zone 5.

## 1. What Are SIPs and How Do They Function?
*Explain manufacturing and structure: Factory-cut panels (typically 4.5" to 8.25" thick for walls, 10.25" to 12.25" for roofs) arrive pre-cut with window openings, door bucks, and electrical chases already routed. Panels are locked together using insulated splines or structural I-beams.*

## 2. Air Leakage & Thermal Performance Advantages
*Explain why stick-built walls leak air: Traditional framing has hundreds of joints between studs, plates, and headers that must be individually caulked. SIPs eliminate almost all interior thermal bridging and achieve sub-1.0 ACH50 airtightness right out of the box with simple spline expansion foam and seam taping.*
